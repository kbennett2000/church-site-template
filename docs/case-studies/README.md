# Case studies

Real churches that have adopted the Church Site Template. These are part marketing, part proof, and part road map for other churches deciding whether to adopt — and for the developers and tech volunteers who help them.

## Featured

- **[Majestic View Church](./majestic-view-church.md)** — Kiowa, Colorado. The inaugural case study and the prototype that became this template.

> **Your church should be here.** Read on for how to submit.

---

## Why case studies matter

For a small church considering this template, a 20-minute decision goes one of two ways:

- **"This looks great in the README but I have no idea if it actually works for a church like ours."** → They don't adopt.
- **"Three churches that look like ours are already running this. I can see their actual sites."** → They adopt.

A case study turns "looks nice" into "I see it working." It also gives you, as the adopting church, a chance to share what worked, what didn't, and what you'd do differently — which helps the next church and helps us improve the template.

## Submitting your case study

After you've launched your site, send a pull request adding your case study to this folder.

### Steps

1. **Copy [`_TEMPLATE.md`](./_TEMPLATE.md)** to a new file in this folder named `your-church-name.md` (lowercase, hyphens, no spaces).
2. **Fill in the template** — most sections are self-explanatory. We'd particularly love:
   - Before/after screenshots (even just the homepage on mobile).
   - One or two specific things the new site lets you do that the old one didn't.
   - A short quote from a staff member.
3. **Add your church to the "Featured" list above** in this README (one bullet, with a link to your case study).
4. **Add yourself to the "Live examples" section** of the [main README](../../README.md#live-examples).
5. **Open a pull request** against the template repo.

We'll review and merge usually within a week. There's no review committee or vetting — if you've launched a site using the template, you're welcome here. We may suggest light editorial polish before merging.

### What we look for

- **Honesty.** What was harder than expected? What would you do differently? Those bits are often more useful to readers than the wins.
- **Specifics.** "We saved time" is fine; "editing a sermon went from 8 minutes to 90 seconds" is better.
- **Photos and screenshots.** Even imperfect ones. Readers want to see what real churches do, not just read about it.
- **Permission to publish.** Make sure your staff and elder board are okay with the case study being public. We'll never publish a church without explicit consent from the church leadership.

### What we don't accept

- Case studies for churches whose site doesn't actually use the template. (If you built a similar site from scratch, that's awesome — but it's not a case study for *this* template.)
- Promotional content disguised as a case study. The audience is other small churches, not your donors.
- Theological or political polemics. Keep the focus on the site and what it does.

## Screenshots

Each case study lives in this folder; its screenshots live at `screenshots/<church-slug>/`. Keep file sizes reasonable (run them through [tinypng.com](https://tinypng.com/) before committing) and aim for the same set of views across case studies so readers can compare:

- Homepage (desktop, ~1440px wide)
- Homepage (mobile, 375px wide)
- One additional page that's important to your church (calendar, ministries, sermons — your choice)
- The CMS dashboard (so other tech volunteers can see what your editors see)

If your old site is still online (or you have archived screenshots), include "before" versions of the homepage — it's the single biggest "wow" comparison.
