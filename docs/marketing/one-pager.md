# Why our church should adopt the Church Site Template

**One-page summary for pastors and church boards considering a website refresh.**

---

## What it is

A free, open-source website template purpose-built for small churches. We adopt it once, customize the branding and content for our church, and from then on our own staff updates the site in their browser — no developer required.

## What we get

- A modern, mobile-first website — looks great on phones (where ~70% of visitors arrive).
- A browser-based editor (TinaCMS) — staff sign in with Google and update sermons, events, staff bios, and service times without touching code.
- A sermon archive, calendar, ministries pages, prayer request form, plan-a-visit page, give page, and contact page — all included.
- Free, modern hosting on Vercel — scales to thousands of visitors a day at no cost.

## What it costs

- **The template itself:** free, forever (MIT-licensed open source).
- **Hosting:** $0/month on Vercel's free tier.
- **GitHub repository:** $0/month (unlimited collaborators).
- **CMS:** $0/month (TinaCloud's free tier covers small-church usage).
- **Custom domain (optional):** ~$12/year if we want `ourchurch.org`.

**Annual total: ~$12.** No per-user fees, no per-edit charges, no escalating pricing tiers.

## What we'd be moving away from

| | Old approach (Wix / Squarespace / WordPress) | Church Site Template |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly cost | $20–$50/month, growing | $0 |
| Annual cost | $240–$600 | ~$12 (domain only) |
| Vendor lock-in | High (content lives in vendor system) | None (content lives in plain files we own) |
| Editor experience | Browser CMS (varies in quality) | Browser CMS (TinaCMS, clean and modern) |
| Mobile experience | Varies by template | Mobile-first by design |
| What if the vendor raises prices? | We pay or migrate (hard) | Doesn't apply — there's no vendor |
| What if we want to redesign in 5 years? | Start over | Edit the open code (or start over — same options) |

## What we'd need to invest

- **One semi-technical volunteer** for ~30 minutes of initial setup, plus ~10 minutes per month for routine maintenance after launch.
- **One or two staff editors** trained on the CMS (about 30 minutes of training each).
- **Content prep:** photos of staff, a hero image of the building, our beliefs statement, our current ministry descriptions. (We already have most of this.)
- **Domain registration** if we don't already own ours.

That's it. No multi-thousand-dollar agency engagement. No recurring contracts.

## What it doesn't do (honestly)

- **It's not a one-click "no setup at all" service.** Adopting it takes ~30 minutes of focused setup by a semi-technical person.
- **There's no built-in 24/7 phone support.** Help comes from the open-source community, the docs, or a developer we hire briefly if we get stuck.
- **It's not a church management system.** Member directories, giving tracking, attendance — those need separate tools (the template integrates with them but doesn't replace them).
- **Major redesigns or new features need a developer.** Day-to-day content is no-code; deep customization isn't.

## Who else uses it

- **[Majestic View Church](https://mvckiowa.com/)** (Kiowa, CO) — the prototype congregation. ~150 weekly attendance. Full case study with before/after screenshots: [docs/case-studies/majestic-view-church.md](../case-studies/majestic-view-church.md)

A small but growing roster of churches has adopted it. Each case study includes specifics on what changed, what was harder than expected, and what they'd do differently.

## What I (the tech volunteer) recommend

I propose we:

1. **Adopt the template** by creating our own copy from GitHub (one click).
2. **Spend a Saturday afternoon** doing initial setup, branding, and photo collection.
3. **Run the new site in parallel** with our current one for a month — pointing visitors to either while we test.
4. **Cut over the domain** once the new site is solid.
5. **Sunset the old site and stop the subscription.**

Total elapsed time: 4–6 weeks. Total project cost: the price of our domain renewal.
