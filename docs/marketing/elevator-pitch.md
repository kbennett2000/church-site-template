# Elevator pitches

Short pitches you can use in 30 seconds (or less) when someone asks "what's this church-site-template thing?"

---

## 30-second pitch (general audience)

> It's a free, open-source website template for small churches. You click "Use this template" on GitHub, customize the church's name, photos, and content, and deploy it for free on Vercel. Editors update the site through a browser-based CMS — no coding. Setup takes about 30 minutes. Ongoing cost: $12 a year for a domain. It exists because small churches deserve a real website without paying $50 a month forever.

## 15-second pitch (busy listener)

> Free, modern church website template. Editors update it in their browser. Free hosting. ~30-minute setup. Built for small churches who can't afford a paid platform — or shouldn't have to.

## One-sentence pitch

> A free, mobile-first website template for small churches — editors update content in their browser, no developer required, deploys free on Vercel.

---

## For a pastor or church leader

> Your church needs a real website. The old options are either pay $30 a month to Squarespace forever, or trust the WordPress install nobody on staff understands. This is a third option: a free template anyone can adopt, that your secretary can update from their phone if they need to, that costs nothing to host, and that you own outright. About 30 minutes to set up. Twelve bucks a year for a domain. That's it.

## For a tech volunteer

> A complete Next.js + TinaCMS church starter. Use-this-template, customize the seed data, push to Vercel. Free tier handles thousands of visitors a day. Editors log in with Google and save changes directly — no GitHub account needed, no approval step. The whole thing is plain Markdown and JSON in your repo — no vendor lock-in. Built so a non-technical staff member can keep it updated after handoff.

## For a developer

> Next.js 16 (App Router, RSC), Tailwind, TinaCMS + TinaCloud. Designed as a GitHub template repo — clicking "Use this template" gives a church their own independent codebase. Content is plain files (Markdown frontmatter via gray-matter, JSON). Every page is statically generated. Free on Vercel's hobby tier. Open source under MIT.

## For someone evaluating it vs. Wix/Squarespace

> Real comparison: Squarespace gets you live in 10 minutes; this gets you live in 30. Squarespace costs $20–30/month forever; this costs $12 a year. Squarespace locks your content into their platform; this puts every byte in a GitHub repo you control. If you can survive 20 extra minutes of setup, the math compounds in your favor immediately.

## For someone worried about being non-technical

> The hard part is one 30-minute session a semi-technical volunteer does once at the beginning. After that, the church secretary, the pastor, anyone — edits sermons and events and staff bios in their browser like they're using Squarespace. There's no terminal, no GitHub, no code in their world. They sign in with their Google account, make a change, hit Save, and it's live in 2 minutes.

## For another developer building open-source

> Open-source church-website starter on Next.js + TinaCMS. Designed around GitHub's "Use this template" feature so adopters get a clean, independent repo (no fork relationship). Generic seed data — clearly placeholder, with `[Replace with…]` markers throughout. Three-track docs (editor / tech volunteer / developer). Inaugural case study and submission template for adopters. MIT.
